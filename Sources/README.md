Background research papers and code bases to direct our initial thoughts on the topic.
PDFs of the papers are added where possible.

# Papers (with code where possible)

| Paper                                                                                                                                      | Repository                                                                                            | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531) (Hinton, 2015)                                            | [Unofficial implementation](https://github.com/a7b23/Distilling-the-knowledge-in-neural-network)      | *The* foundational paper on distillation: training a small model to match the output distribution of a larger model (or ensemble), instead of only matching hard labels. It builds on Buciluǎ et al. (2006), who matched logits directly. Introduces temperature, soft targets, and “dark knowledge” in non-argmax probabilities. |
| [A Comprehensive Survey on Knowledge Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12067) (Mansourian, 2025)                                    | [Official repo](https://github.com/IPL-sharif/KD_Survey/tree/main)                                    | This paper is very comprehensive and detailed but usefully shows a variety of different techniques, algorithms and applications                                                                                                        |
| [Born Again Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04770) (Furlanello, 2018)                                                          | [Unofficial implementation](https://github.com/nocotan/born_again_neuralnet?utm_source=catalyzex.com) | This looks at students that are the same size as their teachers, they tend to outperform the teachers                                                                                                                     |
| [A Survey of On-Policy Distillation for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00626) — Song & Zheng, 2026                      |                                                                                                       | A survey that focuses only on on-policy distillation for LLMs, where the student learns from its own answers instead of only copying the teacher. It argues this is a separate paradigm that earlier surveys missed.      |
| [DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948) — DeepSeek-AI, 2025 | [Official repo](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1)                                           | Mainly about training a reasoning model with RL, but the big model is then used as a teacher and small open models are trained on its written answers. A clean example of classic off-policy distillation at large scale. |
| [Qwen3 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388) — Qwen Team, 2025                                                               | [Official repo](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3)                                                      | The technical report of the Qwen3 models. Their small models are distilled from the big ones in two layers: first they learn from the teacher's answers, then they write their own answers and the teacher corrects them. |
| [On-Policy Distillation](https://www.thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/) — Lu & Thinking Machines Lab, 2025                  | [Official repo](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook)                             | This explains the idea of on-policy distillations clearly and shows it can reach RL-level results with much less compute.                                                                                                 |
| [On-Policy Distillation of Language Models: Learning from Self-Generated Mistakes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13649) — Agarwal et al., 2024 | [Unofficial implementation (TRL GKDTrainer)](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/gkd_trainer) | The paper that introduced on-policy distillation for LLMs (GKD). The student writes its own answers and the teacher scores every token, which fixes the mismatch between training on fixed data and generating at test time. It also shows the best loss (forward KL, reverse KL, JSD) depends on the task. |
| [MiniLLM: Knowledge Distillation of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08543) — Gu et al., 2024 | [Official repo](https://github.com/microsoft/LMOps/tree/main/minillm) | Replaces the usual forward KL with reverse KL, so a small student focuses on the teacher's most likely answers instead of spreading probability onto bad ones. Reverse KL needs samples from the student, so training is on-policy. Most on-policy work, including ours, still uses this loss. |
| [Self-Distilled Reasoner: On-Policy Self-Distillation for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18734) — Zhao et al., 2026 | [Official repo](https://github.com/siyan-zhao/OPSD) | No separate teacher: the same model acts as teacher when it is shown the ground-truth solution, and as student when it isn't. On math benchmarks it beats off-policy distillation and needs far fewer tokens than RL (GRPO). A modern on-policy take on the Born-Again idea. |
| [Rethinking On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models: Phenomenology, Mechanism, and Recipe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13016) — Li et al., 2026 | [Official repo](https://github.com/thunlp/OPD) | Finds on-policy distillation only works when teacher and student reason in compatible ways and the teacher knows something new. A higher benchmark score alone isn't enough, and same-family teachers can look identical to the student. Recommends an off-policy warm start. This backs our headroom test, since a Qwen teacher may add little to a Qwen student. |
| [Specializing Smaller Language Models towards Multi-Step Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12726) — Fu et al., 2023                    | [Official repo](https://github.com/FranxYao/FlanT5-CoT-Specialization)                                | Distils GPT-3.5's math reasoning into small T5 models: big gains on GSM8K, but general skills drop. Its evaluation design (in-distribution, unseen math sets, general benchmarks) is a template for ours.                 |
| [Does Knowledge Distillation Really Work?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05945) — Stanton et al., 2021                                        | [Official repo](https://github.com/samuelstanton/gnosis)                                              | Separates fidelity (how closely the student matches the teacher) from accuracy, and shows students often fail to match even when they could. Gives us a second metric: student-teacher agreement/KL on test.              |
| [Adding Error Bars to Evals: A Statistical Approach to Language Model Evaluations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640) — Miller, 2024        | [Blog post (Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/research/statistical-approach-to-model-evals)       | Treats benchmark questions as a sample, so scores need error bars. Covers paired model comparisons and how many questions are needed. Backs the stats in our headroom test and the on/off-policy comparison.              |
| [Reasoning or Memorization? Unreliable Results of Reinforcement Learning Due to Data Contamination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10532) — Wu et al., 2025 | [Official repo](https://github.com/wumingqi/LLM-Math-Evaluation)                                      | Shows Qwen2.5 models have memorised parts of public math benchmarks, so gains there can be recall, not reasoning. Since we only use Qwen models on a public dataset, it supports checking for contamination.              |
| [A Survey on Knowledge Distillation of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13116) (Xu et al., 2024) | [Official Repo](https://github.com/Tebmer/Awesome-Knowledge-Distillation-of-LLMs) | Comprehensive survey categorizing white-box, black-box, and rationale-based distillation for LLMs to bridge proprietary and open-source models. |
| [Survey on Knowledge Distillation for Large Language Models: Methods, Evaluation, and Application](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01885) (Yang et al., 2024) | — | Comprehensive survey covering LLM distillation pipelines across data synthesis, token-level logit matching, and evaluation metrics like hallucination rates. |
| [A Survey on Model Compression for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07633) (Zhu et al., 2024)| [Community Repo](https://github.com/bupt-ai-club/llm-compression-papers) | Covers knowledge distillation alongside quantization and network pruning, focusing on latency and memory trade-offs in resource-constrained deployment. |
