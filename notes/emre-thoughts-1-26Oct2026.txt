I started with the Mansourian survey. It is huge and maps the whole field,
so we will need more time with it as a group, but it is the right place to
learn the basic mechanisms and vocabulary.

Then I went back to where I had first heard of distillation: DeepSeek-R1
and Qwen3. I find these two very important because they are the state of
the art today. They are the best of the best and accepted choices, and
they use distillation in different ways. DeepSeek keeps it simple: their
big model R1 writes a large set of answers with its reasoning, and smaller
open models are trained only on that text. The students never see the
teacher's probabilities, only what it wrote. Qwen's approach is much more
layered. Their big models go through a long training pipeline of several
stages, and the small models are not trained that way at all; they are
distilled from the big ones. This distillation also has two layers. First
the student learns from the teacher's answers, like DeepSeek. Then the
student writes its own answers and the teacher corrects them token by
token. I find Qwen's success with small models very valuable and
interesting.

After this I wanted another survey, and I found one by Song & Zheng that
focuses only on on-policy methods. On-policy means the student learns from
its own answers instead of only copying the teacher's. I think this is a
topic we have to deal with. DeepSeek shows the classic off-policy way at
its best, Qwen uses on-policy as its second layer, and I also found a
valuable write-up on it by researchers at Thinking Machines Lab.

All this may seem far fetched from what the professor offered us if you
look at the two papers, but what I am checking is only the modern part. It
all started with those papers. With current research and industrial
opportunities changing, the same core ideas are still used, but the focal
point is different.

Making this our focal point would make our efforts relevant and much more
fun. Computation could be a problem, but there are always cloud options.
And because the expectation is only a demonstration, I guess even a laptop
version would be enough. I have an RTX 4060, and I believe we can run the
small Qwen models on it.
